## The words

Agents arrive speaking kanban. The board answers in the vocabulary of the
[north star](/docs/design/): the same five words the CLI, the manifest, and
a catalog use. What a program expects, and what it gets:

<dl class="docs-words">
	<dt>card</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">row</dd><dd class="docs-why">One work the group wants or has. Stable integer <code>id</code>.</dd>
	<dt>column</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">status</dd><dd class="docs-why">There are no columns. A row is <code>want</code> until somebody holds a copy, then <code>have</code>. That is the whole journey.</dd>
	<dt>position</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">desire</dd><dd class="docs-why">1–9. Orders the ledger and is what <code>darsay archive --next</code> reads. The order is deterministic: desire high to low, unrated last, then as added.</dd>
	<dt>external id</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">source</dd><dd class="docs-why">The canonical address is the identity — <code>huggingface:ESCAD/OpenRTLSet</code>, <code>huggingface:datasets/…</code>, or an https home page for a closed work — together with <code>revision</code> and <code>include</code>. Adding is an upsert on it.</dd>
	<dt>tags</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">chips</dd><dd class="docs-why">Read from the work, never written on the card: <code>hints</code> (gated, large, quant, redundant, subset), <code>size_basis</code>, <code>precision</code>, <code>address.kind</code>, and <code>lineage</code> — family, generation, member, read from the name and labeled so. Every size names its scope; GGUF repositories list their actual variants and the size of each.</dd>
	<dt>description</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">note</dd><dd class="docs-why">One plain sentence for why this one. The facts live in the digest.</dd>
	<dt>archive</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">drop</dd><dd class="docs-why">A soft removal: the row leaves the ledger and the catalog, and comes back exactly as it was. <em>Archive</em> means something else here — it is what the CLI does to a work.</dd>
	<dt>delete</dt><dd class="docs-arrow">→</dd><dd class="docs-word">remove</dd><dd class="docs-why">Gone for good. Needs the <code>remove</code> scope; the audit trail keeps what the row was.</dd>
</dl>

Why no tags? *Record, don't fabricate.* A fact on a row comes from upstream
or from the payload, or it is null — and every label is a doorway to the
card that explains it. A free-text tag would be neither. If a chip you
need is missing, it is missing from the model of the models, and that is
where it gets added.

## Choosing a GGUF variant

Start with **Explore collection** in the add form. The collection room
inspects the publication before saving anything, offers three editable
starting points, and explains the encodings, companions, and evidence.
**Review collection** shows the exact revision and selected storage;
**Add this collection** saves the combined selection as one row.
See [Choosing a collection](/docs/board/collections/) for the complete
board and terminal experience.

Expand a row's **GGUF variants** button to compare complete weight sets.
Each size includes every shard of that variant; projectors are separate
companions. **Add variant** creates a new selection row, priced with its
support files, without changing the original row's desire, note, or status.
An existing selection shows **On board**, with **View row** to jump to
it and **Remove variant** to drop that row again; the toast's Undo brings
it back, and vaults are untouched either way. **Copy commands** provides
the exact estimate and archive commands for that selection.

**Refresh size** updates the row's Hub inventory. It does not classify
negatives and prints; use `darsay estimate <board-url>` for that. A
repository total is not one model's size or a classified archive size.

Choose the scope, inspect the artifact's lineage, and then examine recovery
evidence before making a retain/omit decision. A declared parent identifies
another publication to compare; it does not replace this row's source.
`BF16` describes stored precision, not proof of an original checkpoint.
An imatrix marker says an importance matrix was used, not whether its inputs
are private or whether exact recovery is possible. Derived artifacts can
still be worth collecting.

### One model, three collection choices

GLM-5.3-Flash illustrates why scope matters. These measurements refer to
the linked pinned revisions, not a moving branch:

| Collection | Files and payload |
| --- | --- |
| [Unsloth's complete GGUF publication](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/GLM-5.3-Flash-GGUF/tree/2975ab414d30340466d8c51533c6e91f0cca64c1) | 87 files; 2,545,636,747,545 bytes (2.315 TiB). Twelve main variants and two projectors, with no automatic skips. |
| [The publisher's FP8 release](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash/tree/690b705278a3a58e538fcb37c2ca8b5f9511213c) | 72 files; 328,366,172,624 bytes (305.8 GiB). A separate publication with its own identity. |
| Unsloth's `UD-Q4_K_XL` selection at the same pinned revision | 199,707,329,724 bytes (186.0 GiB), including its README. Optional projectors are outside this selection. |

The 2.315 TiB figure describes the complete Unsloth publication, not one
model copy. Choosing the publisher release or one GGUF variant is a new
collection decision. The board preserves the original row until you
explicitly change it, and **Add variant** creates a separate row.

Negative/print terminology alone does not authorize deleting or skipping
an artifact. The automatic skip rule is R15, a verified byte duplicate
with its retained recovery source inside the same bundle. R2 upstream
duplicates remain retained; R9 colocated GGUF weights remain unknown and
retained. Even a classified archive can therefore include many variants.

## Five minutes

Take a board's page address, `https://darsay.io/b/<board>`. Everything
below uses only that.

<Stage label="read it" lines={[
	"curl -s https://darsay.io/b/<board>.json | jq '.entries[] | [.desire, .status, .source]'",
]} />

Add a row. The address is the identity, so sending it twice is safe: the
first answer is `201`, the second `200` with the same `id`.

<Stage label="add a row — an upsert by address" lines={[
	"curl -s -X POST https://darsay.io/api/boards/<board>/entries \\",
	"  -H 'content-type: application/json' \\",
	"  -d '{\"source\": \"ESCAD/OpenRTLSet\", \"desire\": 9, \"note\": \"RTL corpus for training hardware-design models\"}'",
]} />

Apply a whole list. Match by address, add what is missing, update what
differs, leave the identical alone — and see the plan before it lands.

<Stage label="apply a list, dry run first" lines={[
	"curl -s -X POST 'https://darsay.io/api/boards/<board>/apply?dry_run=true' \\",
	"  -H 'content-type: application/json' -d @rows.json",
	"# {\"dry_run\": true, \"summary\": {\"added\": 17, \"updated\": 3, \"unchanged\": 4, ...}, \"rows\": [...]}",
	"curl -s -X POST https://darsay.io/api/boards/<board>/apply \\",
	"  -H 'content-type: application/json' -H 'If-Match: \"184\"' -d @rows.json",
]} />

Hand an agent a key instead of the URL. It opens only this board, only for
the scopes you name, and signs every write with its label.

<Stage label="mint a key, connect an agent" lines={[
	"curl -s -X POST https://darsay.io/api/boards/<board>/keys \\",
	"  -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{\"label\": \"chatgpt\", \"scopes\": [\"write\"]}'",
	"# {\"key\": \"darsay_…\", \"scopes\": [\"read\", \"write\"], \"shown_once\": true, ...}",
	"claude mcp add --transport http darsay https://darsay.io/mcp --header 'Authorization: Bearer darsay_…'",
]} />

The board's ✦ **Agents** panel does the same with buttons, and shows the
recent activity: who did what, with the columns before and after.

## How it stays honest

**One write path.** The page, the CLI's round trip, a key, and the MCP
server all go through the same commit: it bumps the board's `revision`,
stamps `updated` on the row, appends to the audit trail, and tells the
webhooks. There is no door that skips the ledger.

**Revisions.** `GET` answers with `ETag: "184"` and `revision: 184` in the
body. Send it back as `If-Match` and a write is refused with `412 stale` if
anyone — a person, the CLI, another agent — moved the board first.

**Idempotency.** Send `Idempotency-Key: <anything>` with a write. A retry
with the same key and the same request gets the first answer back,
marked `Idempotent-Replayed: true`; a different request under the same
key is refused.

**Dry runs.** `apply` and `batch` take `dry_run: true` and answer with the
plan — what would be added, updated, restored, dropped — without writing
it.

**The audit trail.** Every decision is an event: the action, the actor
(`url`, `cli`, or `key:<label>`), the row, and the decided columns before
and after. The last thousand are kept per board.

**Keys narrow, never widen.** The URL is the capability. A key is the URL
with fewer powers: one board, the scopes `read` · `write` · `claim` ·
`remove`, and no way to delete the board, mint keys, or register
webhooks. A key without `remove` cannot destroy anything.

## What the board is not

It is not a place for bytes: the site never holds weights. It is not an
account: there are no users, only the URL and the keys it mints. And it is
not a kanban: a row has no column to travel to, because the only journey
a work makes is from wanted to held — and that is a fact about somebody's
vault, reported by the CLI or checked off by a person, not a card moved by
hand.
