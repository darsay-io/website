> **In one sentence.** A dataset is the other half of every model: the text it was shaped by, stored as tables of rows, and read by the model only after a tokenizer has cut it into the pieces the model knows.

Models are made from data, tuned with data, and judged on data, and a
workbench that fine-tunes or evaluates needs datasets pinned as carefully
as weights. This page is how a dataset repository is put together, what
happens to its text on the way into a model, and how darsay keeps one.

## What is in a dataset repository

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `data/train-00000-of-00042.parquet` … | The rows, in **Parquet**: a columnar format with a schema, compressed, readable a column at a time. Sharded like weights. |
| `*.jsonl` | One JSON object per line: the format fine-tuning tools read and write, and the easiest to inspect. |
| `*.arrow` | Arrow IPC files: what the `datasets` library caches; memory-mappable. |
| `README.md` | The **dataset card**: what the rows are, where they came from, the licence, and a YAML header naming **configs** and **splits** with their file patterns. |
| `dataset_infos.json` | Row counts, feature types, and sizes per split. |

A **split** is `train`, `validation`, or `test`; a **config** is a
sub-dataset (a language, a subject) with its own splits. The card's
header maps each to its files, and a loader follows the map. A dataset
can be a few kilobytes of prompts or the fifteen-terabyte crawl a
pretraining run consumed; the container is the same.

## From text to tokens

A model never sees text. It sees **token ids**, integers into a
vocabulary, produced by a **tokenizer** that was fitted before the model
was trained and is frozen with it. Modern tokenizers are **byte-pair
encoding** or a cousin: start from bytes, repeatedly merge the most
common adjacent pair into a new token, until the vocabulary has 32,000
to 260,000 entries. Common English words become one token; rare words,
code, and other languages become several; anything at all can be
represented, because bytes are the fallback.

A few consequences you will meet:

- **A token is about three quarters of an English word.** A 15-trillion-token corpus is roughly 11 trillion words, 60 terabytes of text. Context windows are in tokens; so are prices.
- **The tokenizer is part of the model.** Change it and the embedding table means nothing. Two models share a tokenizer only if they were made to (a family, a draft and its target), and that is why speculative decoding needs matching vocabularies.
- **Special tokens** mark structure: beginning of sequence, end of turn, the role markers a chat template emits. They are in the vocabulary and the template; a missing one is why a model "never stops".
- **Tokenization is lossy in surprising places.** Numbers split by digits or by threes; whitespace and case matter; a word at the start of a line may tokenize differently from the same word mid-sentence.

## Quality: deduplication and contamination

The two things that most improve a dataset are removing what is
repeated and removing what is being tested on. **Deduplication**, exact
and near (MinHash over shingles), matters because a passage seen a
hundred times is memorized rather than learned. **Contamination** is
test data in the training set: a benchmark's questions, scraped into
the crawl, make the score meaningless. Careful publishers decontaminate
against the benchmarks they report and say so on the card. For a
fine-tuning set on the bench, the same two habits apply: dedupe your
rows, and keep the held-out tenth out of sight.

## Licences

A dataset's licence is on its card and is separate from any model's.
Text scraped from the web carries its sources' rights whatever the
card says; synthetic data generated by a model carries that model's
terms of use; a dataset assembled from others inherits all of theirs.
For the archive, keep the card and the licence file with the rows: they
are the record of what you may do. Gated datasets ask you to accept
terms before download, exactly like gated models, and `darsay` handles
them the same way.

## Datasets in the vault

<Stage label="a dataset is an ordinary bundle" lines={[
	"darsay estimate datasets/cornell-movie-review-data/rotten_tomatoes   # priced, like a model",
	"darsay archive  datasets/cornell-movie-review-data/rotten_tomatoes   # payload lands under data/",
	"darsay info     cornell-movie-review-data--rotten_tomatoes           # the manifest: files, hashes, card",
]} />

The address is `datasets/owner/name`; the payload lands under `data/`
instead of `model/`; the manifest, the pin, the hashes, and the verbs
are the same. There is no engine: `darsay run` has nothing to run, and
the files are Parquet or JSON Lines that any reader opens. On a board,
a dataset row shows the **dataset** lens and sizes like any other.

## Where this bites

- **Row counts are not sizes.** A million short rows is a few hundred megabytes; a hundred thousand documents can be tens of gigabytes. Price it; darsay does.
- **Parquet is columnar.** Reading one column of one shard is cheap; reading whole rows is what a trainer does. Both are fine; know which you are doing.
- **The tokenizer decides the budget.** A prompt that is 800 tokens in one model's tokenizer can be 1,100 in another's. Measure with the model you will run.
- **Synthetic data has a parent.** A set generated by a model inherits that model's licence and its habits. The card should say which.
